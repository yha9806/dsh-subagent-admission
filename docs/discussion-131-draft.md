I built an independent, experimental lifecycle-admission prototype against DeepSeek Harness commit 47f9438 for the recursive subagent failure shape discussed here. In a bounded, no-model 56-child structural reproduction at the admission seam, a policy-absent Audit baseline allowed all 56 scripted provider starts; the Strict reference policy admitted 4 for one root, denied 5 reachable excess branches before provider execution, and suppressed their 47 descendants.

This is complementary to [dsh-turn-budget](https://github.com/Nunchakus888/dsh-turn-budget), which already provides an immediately installable per-turn circuit breaker through public hooks. The remaining experiment is narrower: one atomic decision shared across direct `ctx.subagents` callers and tool/plugin paths, enforced before provider or Agent materialisation, with capacity retained through the official child lifecycle.

The proposed official surface contains no limits, ledger, root resolver, telemetry, or UI. It is a versioned policy registration whose permit is acquired before provider or Agent materialisation, bound after publication, and released after canonical quiescence. The [repository](https://github.com/yha9806/dsh-subagent-admission) includes a pinned RED/GREEN lifecycle fixture in [reproduction](https://github.com/yha9806/dsh-subagent-admission/blob/main/docs/reproduction.md), an exact-target slim patch, and the proposed [Agent Note](https://github.com/yha9806/dsh-subagent-admission/blob/main/docs/upstream-agent-note.md).
